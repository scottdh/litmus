# Decision: steps, a fourth level under subtasks

Decided by Scott on 2026-11-05, from a live prototype with three working directions on the invented sample map.

**The need:** some subtasks hold a lot of complexity and research, and a review note can't hold a useful breakdown. Grading another level wouldn't help, so this level takes no light.

**Chosen: in the column.** Steps nest under their subtask on the wall, because it works the same way tasks and subtasks already do, so there's nothing new to learn.

How it works:

- A subtask with steps has a count and chevron, and expands in the column. Click its row for the drawer, as with a task. Expand all still only opens tasks, so steps stay closed until asked for.
- A step has a dash rather than a light mark, because anything round could read as a light. It opens in the drawer, with its name and a note. Leaving the note saves it.
- A subtask's drawer lists its steps under its rating, and has Add step, which works like Add subtask and has the same Undo.
- A subtask with steps keeps the light a person gave it. Steps change nothing about a light or a mix, and a note isn't evidence.

Scott's word for this level was sub-subtask. The product says "step", because the longer word is awkward on a button. It's easy to change if it doesn't sit right.

**Moving:**

- A subtask takes its steps with it when it moves among subtasks or to another task.
- It can't move out to a chapter while it has steps, because only subtasks hold them. The drag isn't offered, and Move to leaves out the ends of chapters. Turning steps into subtasks instead would give them lights and lose their notes, and letting tasks hold steps would add a second place to look.
- A step can be reordered among its siblings or moved to any other subtask, and keeps its note. New ones still go at the end.
- Dragging a step shows a line between steps, or an outline round a subtask it can drop into, landing at the end of that subtask's steps. Dropping on an empty subtask gives it its first step. A step can't land among tasks or subtasks, so it never becomes one or takes a light.
- In a step's drawer, Move up and Move down sit under the note, because a step has no light to sit beside. They stay within its subtask. Move to lists every subtask, grouped by chapter. Every move gets the same Undo as any other.
- Dragging a task or subtask still treats an open subtask and its steps as one block, so only steps drop between steps.

**Rejected:**

- **In the drawer** (the wall shows only a count, and steps expand in the drawer with their notes). It kept the wall quietest and kept notes one click away, but you can't see a breakdown from the map or compare two, and it behaves differently from the levels above.
- **As a brief** (a subtask opens a 760 px reading page with every step and note on show). The best for reading research, but it squeezes the map to about two chapters at 1440 px, and adds a second kind of panel.

**Known costs:**

- The column is narrow by the third indent, so long step names run to five or six lines.
- Notes are a click further away than in either rejected direction, in a drawer that doesn't show the subtask's light or review.
- Move to names a subtask without its task, so two subtasks with the same name in one chapter look the same there.
